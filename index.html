Neural Field Ensembles for Aerodynamic Surface Prediction: Winning Solution to the ONERA CRM Wall Distribution 2025 Challenge

Lionel Salesses, Caroline Sainvitu, Tariq Benamara
Cenaero
2026
Paper arXiv

Abstract

Machine-learning surrogate models offer a promising alternative to high-fidelity Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations for aerodynamic analysis and design. However, constructing accurate surrogates for realistic aircraft configurations remain challenging due to complex geometries, multiple flow regimes, and limited training data. This work presents the methodology that achieved first place in the ONERA CRM Wall Distribution Regression Challenge, which focuses on predicting pressure and skin-friction coefficient distributions over the NASA Common Research Model wing-body-pylon-nacelle configuration under different operating conditions. The proposed approach formulates the problem as a conditional neural field mapping spatial coordinates, surface normals, and operating conditions to aerodynamic wall quantities. Fourier feature encoding, a relative squared error objective aligned with the challenge metric, ensemble learning, and k-fold cross-validation are progressively introduced to improve prediction accuracy and exploit the limited training data. Beyond presenting the final methodology, the paper documents the successive model design choices that led to the winning solution through a comprehensive ablation study and discusses several alternative approaches that were investigated but ultimately discarded. On the hidden competition test set, the proposed methodology achieves an overall score of 8.81, outperforming the strongest organizer-provided baseline, which achieved a score of 8.64, while requiring approximately three orders of magnitude fewer trainable parameters. These results illustrate that carefully designed coordinate-based neural fields constitute an efficient and robust framework for aerodynamic surrogate modeling on complex geometries under limited-data conditions.

The ONERA CRM Wall Distribution Challenge

High-fidelity CFD simulations are essential for aerodynamic design, but their computational cost makes them impractical for applications requiring a large number of evaluations. The ONERA CRM Wall Distribution Regression Challenge investigates whether machine-learning surrogate models can accurately reproduce aerodynamic wall quantities at a fraction of the computational cost of CFD.

The benchmark is based on the NASA/Boeing Common Research Model Wing-Body-Pylon-Nacelle (CRM-WBPN) configuration. The task is to predict the pressure coefficient and the three components of the skin-friction coefficient over the aircraft surface from the surface geometry and aerodynamic operating conditions.

The challenge is particularly demanding because the dataset combines a complex three-dimensional geometry, multiple aerodynamic regimes, localized flow phenomena such as shocks and separation, and a limited number of available CFD simulations.

260,774 surface points per simulation   |   468 RANS simulations   |   312 training configurations   |   146 hidden test configurations

An additional difficulty is that the CFD mesh used to generate the simulations is not provided. Only surface coordinates, surface normals, aerodynamic conditions, and wall quantities are available. This prevents the direct use of methods relying on mesh connectivity and motivates a mesh-independent representation.

Why is the Problem Difficult?

Complex geometry

The CRM-WBPN combines the wing, fuselage, pylon, and nacelle into a complex three-dimensional surface that cannot be naturally represented on a regular Cartesian grid.

Limited data

Only 312 simulations are available for model development, despite the large variability of the aerodynamic flow fields.

Multiple flow regimes

The database spans Mach numbers from 0.3 to 0.96 and angles of attack from -15° to 15°, including transonic shocks, flow separation, and configurations approaching stall.

Multiscale fields

The target fields contain both smooth regions and highly localized variations. In particular, pressure fields may exhibit sharp transonic shocks, while skin-friction fields contain abrupt variations associated with complex flow phenomena.

The competition also imposed a limit of 20 submissions to the hidden test set. Reliable local validation was therefore essential, while reserving validation data necessarily reduced the amount of data available for training.

Proposed Methodology

Rather than introducing a new neural architecture, we investigate how existing machine-learning techniques can be combined effectively for this challenging low-data aerodynamic surrogate-modeling problem.

We formulate aerodynamic field prediction as a conditional neural field. The model takes the spatial coordinates and surface normal of a point together with the aerodynamic operating conditions and predicts the four wall quantities at that location: Cp, Cf,x, Cf,y, and Cf,z.

Mesh-independent representation

Because the CFD mesh is unavailable, the model operates directly on the point-wise representation of the aircraft surface. No mesh connectivity or manually constructed surface parameterization is required.

Fourier feature encoding

Standard MLPs tend to learn low-frequency components preferentially. To improve the representation of localized flow structures, the Cartesian coordinates are transformed using Fourier feature encoding before being provided to the neural network.

Metric-aligned training

The challenge score combines global accuracy with a worst-case relative error. We therefore replace the conventional mean squared error with a relative squared error objective, encouraging the model to reduce relative prediction errors across the different flow configurations.

Ensemble learning

Multiple neural fields are trained independently and their predictions are averaged. This reduces the variance associated with individual models and improves robustness, particularly for difficult configurations.

K-fold cross-validation

Finally, the available 312 simulations are partitioned into five folds. Models trained on the different folds are combined into the final ensemble, allowing every available simulation to contribute to model training while increasing ensemble diversity.

From a Simple Neural Field to the Winning Solution

The final solution was not obtained from a single architectural innovation. Instead, it emerged through a progressive development process in which each component was evaluated experimentally.

Starting from a simple coordinate-based MLP, we progressively introduced Fourier feature encoding, increased model capacity, a relative squared error loss, ensemble learning, and finally a k-fold cross-validation ensemble.

Final Results

The final model achieved a score of 8.81 on the hidden competition test set, ranking first among all submitted approaches. The strongest organizer-provided baseline, a Global MLP, obtained a score of 8.64.

Importantly, the neural-field approach achieves this performance with substantially fewer trainable parameters than the Global MLP baseline. The final solution consists of an ensemble of neural fields, with approximately 73 million parameters in total, compared with approximately 17 billion parameters for the Global MLP.

Quantitative performance

On the hidden test set, the final methodology reaches an overall R2 of 0.973 and a worst-case relative mean absolute error of 0.210. Improvements are observed across the four predicted quantities, with the largest remaining errors associated with difficult flow configurations and localized spatial structures.

Aerodynamic Field Predictions

The predictions remain in close agreement with the reference CFD solutions over most of the aircraft surface. The largest discrepancies are concentrated in regions with strong spatial gradients, including transonic shocks and regions affected by flow separation.

Key Takeaways

  • Coordinate-based modeling provides a natural mesh-independent representation for irregular aerodynamic surfaces.
  • Fourier feature encoding substantially improves the representation of localized and high-frequency flow structures.
  • Metric-aligned training is important when the evaluation criterion strongly penalizes worst-case configurations.
  • Ensembling improves robustness by reducing the influence of individual model failures.
  • K-fold cross-validation allows all available CFD simulations to contribute to training while providing a diverse set of models for the final ensemble.
  • A carefully designed combination of established techniques can outperform a much larger global regression model without requiring a fundamentally new neural architecture.

Data & Reproducibility

The CRM-WBPN database used in the challenge has been publicly released following the competition. The dataset provides the CFD-derived aerodynamic wall quantities used to develop and evaluate surrogate models.
